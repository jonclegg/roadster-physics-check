# Methods

This is every equation, constant and assumption behind the [Roadster Physics Check](https://wooten-labs.com/roadster/), written to match [`site/roadster/physics.js`](../site/roadster/physics.js). If this document and the code disagree, the code is what runs; please open an issue.

The numbers this produces at the default hardware, how they compare with the video, and how each dial changes them are in [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md). That file is generated by `node scripts/roadster_check.mjs`.

**What this can and can't tell you.** It asks one question: with the hardware Tesla described, could a car fly the motion the video shows? It can't tell you whether the footage is real. A "Plausible" verdict means the simulated car, flying a program fitted to the video, makes it with these assumptions. It doesn't mean the video is genuine.

## 2. Telemetry read off the broadcast

During the ramp runs and the kickflip, Tesla's broadcast shows live gauges: altitude (ft), G, pitch (degrees) and fan downforce (kg). We stepped through the stream at 0.1–1 s intervals and wrote down every reading. They are the arrays `WALL_TELEMETRY`, `DROP_TELEMETRY` and `KICKFLIP_TELEMETRY` in `physics.js`, and the gold dots on the page's charts.

- **The altitude gauge lags.** On both flights the G gauge spikes about 0.25 s before the altitude trace bends. A rear shot also shows the car clearly airborne while the gauge still reads 0.0 ft. Altitude readings are shifted 0.25 s earlier.
- **On the wall ride the altitude gauge also drifts.** It still reads 18 ft with the car parked at the bottom. There the car is placed using the pitch gauge and the ramp's shape instead.
- **Heights are of the car's floor**, so a parked car reads 0 whichever way up it is. Upside down at the lip that gives 67 ft, against the gauge's 68.
- **The tower flip has no gauges.** Heights were measured in pixels using an assumed 2 m car width for scale (`TOWER_MEASURED`). The camera cuts away mid-flight, so the apex isn't measurable. Treat that stunt as roughly ±30%.

### The ramp

Pitch against altitude fits a loop of radius 9.5–10.6 m, depending on how the gauge lag is handled. We use $R = 10.5$ m, running from flat to 170°. That puts the lip about 21 m (69 ft) up, where the gauge settles. The ramp surface is

```math
x = R\sin\theta, \qquad y = R(1 - \cos\theta)
```

where $\theta$ is the car's position angle on the loop, equal to its pitch.

## 5. The tank

### Gas on board

```math
m_{\text{gas}} = \rho(p_{\text{fill}},\ 293.15\ \text{K}) \cdot V
```

$\rho$ comes from CoolProp's reference equation of state for nitrogen, not the ideal-gas law. At 7,000 psi, $\rho = 411.9$ kg/m³, so $m_{\text{gas}} = 247.2$ kg. The tables are generated by [`scripts/roadster_tank_tables.py`](../scripts/roadster_tank_tables.py).

### Blowdown

**Adiabatic (the default).** Gas leaves faster than heat can flow in from the walls, so what remains expands along an isentrope:

```math
s(\rho, T) = s(\rho_{\text{fill}}, T_{\text{fill}})
```

CoolProp gives $p(\rho)$ and $T(\rho)$ along that isentrope. The page interpolates them with a monotone cubic. The table stops where the isentrope reaches the saturation line, because past that point the gas would start to liquefy. Gas below that density is stranded: from 7,000 psi it ends at 68.2 kg/m³, about 41 kg that can't be used, and the gas is near −160 °C by then.

**Isothermal (the best case).** $T$ stays at 293.15 K and $p(\rho)$ follows the real-gas isotherm.

### Pressure at the nozzles

```math
p_0 = \begin{cases}\min(p_{\text{tank}},\ p_{\text{reg}}) & \text{with a regulator}\\ p_{\text{tank}} & \text{without}\end{cases}
```

### Exhaust speed

This is ideal isentropic expansion from $p_0$ and the tank temperature $T$ down to ambient, scaled by the efficiency $\eta$:

```math
v_e = \eta\sqrt{2\,c_p\,T\left[1 - \left(\frac{p_a}{p_0}\right)^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}\right]}
```

Full tank, 1,000 psi regulator, $\eta = 0.8$: $v_e = 0.8\sqrt{2 \cdot 1039 \cdot 293.15 \cdot 0.7005} = 523$ m/s.

### Thrust available

Each nozzle's limit scales with the feed pressure once the tank drops below its design pressure:

```math
F_{\max} = F_{\text{nozzle}} \cdot \min\left(1,\ \frac{p_0}{p_{\text{design}}}\right), \qquad p_{\text{design}} = \min(p_{\text{reg}},\ p_{\text{fill}})
```

With a regulator, thrust stays at full strength until the tank falls below the regulator pressure. Without one, it fades from the first second. Once only stranded gas is left, $F_{\max} = 0$.

### Gas use

```math
\dot m = \frac{F_{\text{total}}}{v_e}, \qquad F_{\text{total}} = \sum F_{\text{corner}} + \sum F_{\text{rear}} + |F_{\text{yaw}}|
```

The burned gas comes off the car's mass at every step.

## 6. The car as a rigid body

The body frame has the nose along $+x$, the roof along $+y$ and the right side along $+z$, with the origin at the centre of gravity. Mass changes as the gas burns: $m = m_{\text{dry}} + m_{\text{gas}}(t)$.

```math
I = m \cdot \mathrm{diag}(k_{\text{roll}}^2,\ k_{\text{yaw}}^2,\ k_{\text{pitch}}^2)
```

### Forces and moments from the nozzles

With corner thrusts $F_i$ at $(x_i, y_i, z_i)$, total rear thrust $F_r$ and yaw-jet force $F_y$:

```math
\mathbf F_{\text{body}} = \left(F_r,\ \sum F_i,\ F_y\right)
```

```math
M_{\text{roll}} = -\sum z_i F_i, \qquad M_{\text{yaw}} = -x_{\text{yaw}} F_y, \qquad M_{\text{pitch}} = \sum x_i F_i + 0.15\,F_r
```

Every push makes a turning moment as well as a force. So a roll comes from one side pushing harder than the other, and that also pushes the car up.

### Equations of motion

```math
m\,\dot{\mathbf v} = R(q)\,\mathbf F_{\text{body}} - m g\,\hat{\mathbf y}
```

```math
I\,\dot{\boldsymbol\omega} = \mathbf M - \boldsymbol\omega \times \left(I\boldsymbol\omega + \mathbf H_w\right), \qquad \dot q = \tfrac12\, q \otimes (\boldsymbol\omega, 0)
```

$\mathbf H_w = (0,\ 0,\ -J_w\Omega)$ is the angular momentum of the wheels and motor rotors, which keep spinning after lift-off at $\Omega = v_{\text{take-off}}/0.36$ m. When the car rolls, this gyroscope swings the nose sideways, and the yaw jets fire to cancel it. In the kickflip that costs about 0.3 kN of yaw thrust and 1.2 kg of gas.

Integration is semi-implicit Euler at 240 Hz, recorded at 60 Hz. On the road, the motors hold the program's speed and the road carries the weight until vertical thrust exceeds it, at which point the car is airborne.

## 7. The flight computer

The car is never moved along the video's path. Each stunt is a program for a flight computer, read off the video: where to lift off, how hard to burn, how high to go, which roll to fly and how to land. The computer sees only the car's state and the thrust it has right now.

### Holding an attitude

```math
\mathbf M_{\text{wanted}} = I\left(\omega_n^2\,\mathbf e + 2\zeta\omega_n(\boldsymbol\omega_{\text{ref}} - \boldsymbol\omega) + \boldsymbol\alpha_{\text{ref}}\right)
```

$\mathbf e$ is the rotation vector from the current attitude to the target. $\boldsymbol\omega_{\text{ref}}$ and $\boldsymbol\alpha_{\text{ref}}$ are the rate and acceleration of a manoeuvre being followed, such as a roll.

### Sharing the work between the four corner nozzles

Nozzles only push, and each makes at most $F_{\max}$. The moments are served first and lift gets what's left:

```math
s_i = -\frac{M_{\text{roll}}\,z_i}{4 z^2} + \frac{M_{\text{pitch}}\,x_i}{4 x^2}
```

If the spread $\max s_i - \min s_i$ exceeds $F_{\max}$, every $s_i$ is scaled down to fit. Then

```math
F_i = \mathrm{clamp}\left(b + s_i,\ 0,\ F_{\max}\right), \qquad b = \mathrm{clamp}\left(\tfrac{L}{4},\ -\min s_i,\ F_{\max} - \max s_i\right)
```

where $L$ is the lift wanted. The yaw jets take the yaw moment:

```math
F_y = \mathrm{clamp}\left(-\frac{M_{\text{yaw}}}{x_{\text{yaw}}},\ \pm 0.25\,F_{\max}\right)
```

### Rolling

A roll is a trapezoid: spin up, hold a steady rate, spin down. Its acceleration is capped by what one side's two nozzles can deliver right now, with a 40% reserve:

```math
\alpha \le \min\left(\alpha_{\text{plan}},\ 0.6 \cdot \frac{2 F_{\max}\, z}{I_{\text{roll}}}\right)
```

### Landing burn

The computer plans a descent speed it can still stop from:

```math
v_d(h) = -\sqrt{v_b(h)^2 + 2a\,\max(0,\ h - h_{\text{catch}})}, \qquad a = \max\left(0.3,\ \min\left(a_{\text{plan}},\ 0.85\left(\frac{4F_{\max}\cos\phi}{m} - g\right)\right)\right)
```

$\phi$ is the tilt from vertical, and $v_b$ eases from a let-down speed to the touchdown speed over the last 0.6 m. The car coasts until it is falling faster than $v_d$, then commands

```math
L = \frac{m\left(8\,(v_d - v_y) + v_d'(h)\,v_y + g\right)}{\cos\phi}
```

Because $a$ shrinks to what the nozzles can actually give, a weaker or heavier car starts braking higher up. That costs more gas.

### Steering

The lift vector may lean up to 15° to steer horizontally, fading to level over the last 1.5 m so the car lands flat.

## 8. Each stunt's program

| | Kickflip (11:12) | Tower flip (21:53) | Wall drop (7:10) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start | Rolling at 6.2 m/s, lift off at the lineup | Parked, lift off at 21:53.6 | Climb the loop to the 170° lip, following the altitude trace |
| Boost | 14 m/s² above gravity (2.4 G felt) until a ballistic climb reaches 8.0 m | 3 g above gravity (4 G felt) to 7.2 m | — |
| Roll | Full 360° at up to 5.0 rad/s, 25 rad/s² | Full 360° at up to 4.5 rad/s, 25 rad/s² | Coast upside down 0.35 s, then a half roll at up to 3.5 rad/s, 12 rad/s² |
| Landing | Plans on 6.5 m/s², treats 2.0 m as the ground until past the parked cars, touches down at 0.6 m/s | Plans on 18 m/s², touches down at 0.6 m/s | Catch at 3 m planning on 17.8 m/s², let down at 1.5 m/s, touch down at 0.3 m/s. The catch may start in the last 30° of the roll. |

The kickflip's boost stops early by the height the roll will add. Spinning up and down each push the car up by about $I_{\text{roll}}\omega/(zm)$, and half of that counts. Without this, it overshoots the apex.

## 9. The wall ride: fans and grip

The car drives the loop, following the angle trace from the pitch gauge. Its target speed is $v_{\text{ref}} = R\dot\theta$.

**Road push** (normal force), with fan suction $S$:

```math
N = \frac{m v^2}{R} + S + m g\cos\theta
```

If $N < 0$ the car leaves the ramp and falls with nothing firing.

**Drive** needed to follow the video, against what grip and the motors allow:

```math
F_{\text{need}} = m\left(a_{\text{ref}} + 4(v_{\text{ref}} - v)\right) + m g\sin\theta, \qquad |F_{\text{drive}}| \le \min(\mu N,\ 32.7\ \text{kN})
```

If the need exceeds that, the tyres slip and the car falls behind the video.

**Suction** is the broadcast's downforce gauge reading times $g$, capped by what 80 kW of fans can hold. Air leaks in under the skirts through the gap $\delta$:

```math
Q = C_d\,P_s\,\delta\sqrt{\frac{2\Delta p}{\rho_{\text{air}}}}, \qquad P_{\text{fan}} = \frac{\Delta p\,Q}{\eta_f}
```

Solving for the largest pressure drop at 80 kW:

```math
\Delta p_{\max} = \left(\frac{P_{\text{fan}}\,\eta_f}{C_d\,P_s\,\delta\sqrt{2/\rho_{\text{air}}}}\right)^{2/3}, \qquad S_{\max} = \Delta p_{\max}\,A
```

At $\delta = 3$ mm, $S_{\max} \approx 7{,}370$ kg. At its worst moment the wall ride needs about 2,300 kg, so the fans run out at about 16 mm. The ride fails if the car leaves the ramp or falls more than 15° behind the video.

## 10. The 0–60 run

The tyres and the four rear nozzles push together. The fans suck the car down with $S = \min(2mg,\ 4{,}700\ \text{kg} \cdot g)$, Tesla's "2 G of suction":

```math
F_{\text{tyre}} = \min\left(32.7\ \text{kN},\ \frac{1\ \text{MW}}{v},\ \mu \cdot \tfrac12(mg + S)\right)
```

The ½ is a conservative share of the load on the tyres that can drive. The steady rear thrust needed to reach 60 mph in 0.81 s is found by bisection, burning real gas from the tank as it goes.

## 11. Landing, crashing and the verdicts

**Contact.** 27 points on the body (a 3 × 3 × 3 grid over the car's length, height and width) are checked every step.

- If any point is inside a parked car's box, the car hits it.
- When the lowest point reaches the road (after 0.2 s in the air), the car has landed if its roof is within 25° of up and it is coming down at 3 m/s or less.
- Otherwise it crashes: on its roof, tipped over, or too hard.

**Verdicts.**

| Stunt | Implausible | Barely plausible | Plausible |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kickflip, tower flip, wall drop | Doesn't land | Lands, but uses over 85% of the usable gas, or needs all its thrust for over 0.1 s | Lands with margin |
| Wall ride | Falls off or slides back down | Holds on, but the tyres slip for over 0.2 s, the fans max out, or suction margin is under 1.3× | Holds on with margin |
| 0–60 | Slower than 0.86 s even at full thrust | Needs over 85% of rear thrust, or slower than 0.82 s | Makes 0.81 s with margin |

## 12. Known limitations

These are the places most likely to change an answer, roughly in order:

1. **The car's weight.** It moves every result. Moravy's "just under 1,700 kilos" doesn't say whether the air is included. If it isn't, the jumps get much harder; see the `dryMass` sweep in [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md).
2. **Stiff skirts on a curved ramp.** The fan model assumes the skirts flex to follow the loop. The floor is flat and the loop curves, so between the axles a stiff skirt would sit roughly 8 cm off the ramp. Through a gap like that, 80 kW of fans can't hold the car. This is probably the strongest physics argument against the wall ride as shown.
3. **Exhaust efficiency** lumps together nozzle losses, cooling through the regulator and condensation late in the blowdown. The 80% default is a judgement.
4. **The programs are fitted to the video.** The boost, apex and roll rates come from the footage, and the landing controller is ours. A cleverer controller might use less gas, and a clumsier one more.
5. **Not modelled:** aerodynamic drag (speeds are low), ground effect and plume interaction near the road, and nozzle response time. The wheels are assumed to spin freely after lift-off; braking them would remove most of the yaw load.
6. **Telemetry by eye.** The readings come from a broadcast overlay at 0.1–1 s steps, with a lagging altitude gauge. The tower flip comes from pixel measurements (about ±30%).
7. **Geometry is estimated.** Nozzle positions, inertia and the Roadster's dimensions are our estimates, because Tesla hasn't published them.
